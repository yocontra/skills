# House style for upward 1:1 prep

The writing rules: what to do with the gathered activity and any
answers to the closing questions.

## Top-level rule: every bullet implies an action

An upward prep section isn't a list of things on the user's mind —
it's a list of things they want the meeting to *do*. Every bullet
should imply: "by the end of this 1:1, I want X to be different."

If the answer to "what changes after the meeting?" is "nothing, I
just want them to know," that's an FYI.

## Concrete vs vague

| Vague | Concrete |
|-------|----------|
| "The billing migration" | "Billing migration: the platform team is blocking on a sign-off from my manager's manager. Need: ask in this meeting whether they can escalate." |
| "Concerns about <person>" | "<person>: missed the last two ship dates, has gone heads-down. Asking: have you noticed the same? What's your read?" |
| "Want to grow as a manager" | "I'm wrestling with whether to delegate the Foo migration to <person> or run it myself. Want your read on the trade-off." |
| "Things are weird with the Bar team" | "The Bar team rejected our last two integration approaches. Asking: is there an org-level conversation needed?" |

The pattern: **name the thing**, **name the people**, **name what's
different by next week**.

## "Would you say this out loud?" test

Especially in the Team section — read the bullet aloud. Would the user
say that exact sentence to their manager's face? If not, the wording is
wrong even if the underlying concern is real.

Wrong:
- "<person> is checked out and I don't know if they want to be here."

Right:
- "<person>: signal seems off — missed two ship dates, less in 1:1s.
  Asking your read."

The first is judgment without evidence. The second is the same concern
reframed as a question. Reframe judgments to questions when you can.

## Decision vs Ask vs FYI

- **Decision Needed** — the user has a choice to make and wants the
  manager involved in choosing.
- **Ask** — the user has decided what they want; the manager needs to
  *do* something.
- **FYI** — the user isn't asking for action; they want them informed.

Test: "What do you want from this person in this meeting?"

- "Help me decide" → Decision
- "Do this" / "Authorize this" / "Push on this" → Ask
- "Just know" → FYI

## Naming people in the doc

Private prep doc, so naming is fine. But:

- Use full names or first-name+last-initial, not nicknames or usernames.
- No speculation about people's private lives.
- No verbatim team quotes unless the user explicitly wants them
  shared. Paraphrase by default.

## Length guidance

Keep each section short enough to say out loud in about a minute. A
bullet that carries two ideas is two bullets.

| Section | Typical |
|---------|---------|
| Wins | 1-3 short bullets |
| Decisions Needed | 0-3 entries |
| Asks | 0-4 entries |
| Team | 0-3 entries |
| Personal / Growth | 0-2 entries |
| FYI | 0-3 entries |

Empty sections are fine. **Don't backfill content.** An upward prep
that's mostly empty because the week was clean is a successful one.

## What this doc does not do

- Doesn't tell the user how to handle a team / people issue.
- Doesn't pre-write feedback.
- Doesn't predict the manager's response.
- Doesn't draft prose beyond bullet-level framing.

The user is responsible for knowing what to say. The skill makes sure
they walked in remembering everything they intended to say.

## Anti-patterns to refuse

Push back during Edit if a draft includes:

- **Performative wins.** "Shipped Foo (great work by the team!)" —
  the boss read the weekly. Headlines only.
- **Vague concerns about specific people.** "<person> seems off."
  Either name the operational concern or skip it.
- **Asks without owners.** "Need air cover on the Bar push." From
  whom? For what?
- **Aspirational decisions.** "Need to decide on the future of X"
  with no deadline. Either a real decision (with a forcing function)
  or an FYI.
- **Items that belong in the weekly.** "Closed 12 bugs this week."
  The weekly already says this.
